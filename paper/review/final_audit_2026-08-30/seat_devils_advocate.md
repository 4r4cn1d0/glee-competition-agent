# Devil's Advocate Final Audit

## Verdict

**Reject in its exact current form.** The current package has three submission-stopping conditions: the core PDF configuration omits the mandatory NeurIPS checklist, the single-blind author block is still a placeholder, and the manuscript itself records an unresolved human-data ethics/data-use determination as a precondition to submission. The first two are manuscript or packaging fixes. The ethics determination is an external precondition and cannot be repaired by softer wording.

After those conditions are cleared, the strongest merits verdict remains **borderline reject / major revision**. The objective derivation, bounded empirical wording, and key policy-code correspondences are substantially sound. The remaining scientific weakness is narrower but central: the paper proposes an audit contract without an end-to-end final-policy conformance denominator, a production-equivalent review artifact, or evidence that use of the contract changes behavior or competitive performance. The work is therefore a careful single-system forensic case study, not yet a validated general method.

## Strongest rejection case

The strongest defensible rejection is not that the paper's rank objective is mathematically wrong, nor that it hides a failed positive experiment. Those objections do not survive the current text. The rejection case is that the claimed methodological contribution is evaluated primarily by the records generated by the same instrumentation whose adequacy is at issue.

The manuscript says the audit contract links an official objective, source, policy assets, runtime decisions, complete cohorts, and bounded claims (`paper/main.tex:61-84`, `119-127`). Yet for the submitted final era it can establish only that 63 games completed, 787 local actions were returned, no local error was logged, and official count increments reconciled (`paper/main.tex:204-251`). It expressly cannot establish action-level final-policy conformance, service receipt, placement, or the process-to-commit-to-server chain (`paper/main.tex:243-250`). The review artifact likewise cannot re-audit raw joins, reconstruct randomized assignment, replay opponent responses, prove production equivalence, or verify service receipt and result association (`paper/review_artifact/REPRODUCIBILITY_BOUNDARY.md:16-27`).

That limitation matters because the methodological novelty is auditability itself. Mathematical identities and frozen hashes show internal consistency, but not that the deployed system executed the claimed policy end to end. Separate bargaining and simulation cohorts demonstrate selected code paths, not conformance of all 787 final-era actions (`paper/main.tex:231-241`). No with-audit versus without-audit comparison, cross-competition replication, or independent provenance check shows that the proposed contract improves reliability or scientific inference. The claim matrix appropriately leaves the audit and transfer claims only partly supported, and `paper/review/claim_evidence_validation.json` explicitly validates schema/alignment rather than evidence truth. On scholarly merit, a reviewer can therefore judge the contribution as a well-documented postmortem and engineering checklist whose general methodological claims outrun its validation.

## Critical issues

### C1. Mandatory checklist is excluded from the submission configuration

**Class: fatal blocker; manuscript-fixable.**

The core build sets `\technicalappendixfalse` and `\paperchecklistfalse` (`paper/main.tex:29-39`), and `paper/main_core.tex:1-4` selects that core path. `paper/README.md:18-21` calls the resulting PDF the exact submission candidate, while `paper/README.md:31-37` says the checklist is internal only. This directly conflicts with the retained official template, which says not to remove the checklist and warns that papers without it will be desk rejected (`paper/official_template/checklist.tex:3-5`). The current official [NeurIPS Paper Checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist) likewise requires the checklist in the single PDF and says it does not count toward the page limit.

This is fatal only to the current package, not to the research. Enable the checklist in the core PDF, leave the technical appendix out if required, repair its references, and rebuild.

### C2. Human-data ethics and data-use status is explicitly unresolved

**Class: fatal blocker; external precondition.**

The manuscript states that human and agent games cannot be separated in the available aggregates and that an applicable ethics/data-use determination is required before submission (`paper/main.tex:325-335`). The private checklist answers the Code of Ethics item "No" and says the draft must not be submitted until review is resolved (`paper/checklist.tex:124-127`); its human-subjects answers remain unresolved as well (`paper/checklist.tex:190-204`). The official competition documentation confirms that the shared pool may include human players and exposes only incomplete opponent-type information (`docs/reference/glee-docs.md:451-461`).

Withholding raw messages and identities is a sound privacy control, and the aggregate artifact privacy scan passed. It does not itself settle whether analysis and publication of mixed human-agent interaction records requires organizer permission, exemption, or institutional review. The authors need a documented determination and any required organizer approval before submission. This cannot be waived by calling the release aggregate-only.

### C3. The current single-blind submission is not attributable

**Class: fatal packaging blocker; manuscript-fixable, with external profile/form checks.**

The call is single-blind (`docs/reference/glee-docs.md:540-568`), but the author block remains `Author Name`, `Affiliation`, and a placeholder address (`paper/main.tex:52-56`; also flagged at `paper/README.md:44`). The actual author identity and affiliation must be inserted. OpenReview profile completeness and the required public agent ID are submission-form preconditions, not manuscript claims, but must also be verified.

## Major issues

### M1. The Agent Behavior Analysis does not measure final-policy conformance

The required section is present and commendably separates designed rules, observed local behavior, and unknown conformance (`paper/main.tex:204-251`). Its complete 63-game census avoids a completed-deal denominator. However, it never reports the central alignment quantity: among the 787 returned actions, how many matched the frozen submitted policy owner and action under the exact observed state? The bargaining `153/153` rule-change result comes from a pooled 25-game cohort, and the negotiation `6,188/6,188` selection result comes from simulation (`paper/main.tex:231-241`). Neither is a final-era, all-family conformance audit.

This is not a hidden flaw because the paper states it. It is still materially score-changing for a call that requires Agent Behavior Analysis and asks how alignment was ensured. Either add an independently auditable final-era conformance denominator and deployment provenance, or narrow the claim from an audit contract to a partial local observability protocol.

### M2. The endpoint does not validate the final policy profile

The public pre-close endpoint covers 46,383 games across five sequential profiles, while the submitted final configuration covers only 63 games and receives roughly 3.7% to 4.9% of the exponentially weighted family rating (`paper/main.tex:132-149`, `256-283`). The registry also reports that none of 25 live-reached interventions passed its precommitted efficacy rule (`paper/main.tex:275-283`). Consequently, rank 2/152 is strong evidence about the adaptive account trajectory, but weak evidence that the final profile or any named intervention caused the result.

The manuscript now says this correctly, so this is not causal misreporting. It is a limitation of what empirical contribution remains: the final system is lightly observed, and the long endpoint is selected over an adaptive, best-of-five history. Acceptance must rest on the methodological case rather than efficacy of the submitted policy, and that methodological case has the validation gap in C1/M1.

### M3. Review-time reproducibility is not actually delivered

The core says a verification bundle was prepared (`paper/main.tex:325-329`), but the release plan says the review bundle remains local unless an organizer-approved channel exists (`paper/ARTIFACT_RELEASE_PLAN.md:23-26`). The submission README similarly describes a one-PDF workflow without a separate artifact upload (`paper/README.md:63-71`). A reviewer receiving only the core PDF has no route to run the checks on which many table values and policy-code correspondences depend.

Even if distributed, the artifact is intentionally not production-equivalent and excludes the raw joins, assignment identifiers/salts, service receipts, opponent messages, and full deployment manifests needed for independent end-to-end verification (`paper/review_artifact/REPRODUCIBILITY_BOUNDARY.md:16-27`). Those exclusions are often privacy-justified, but the paper must distinguish "authors prepared a local verifier" from "reviewers can reproduce the evidence." Provide an approved access route and a privacy-safe provenance object, or remove any implication of review-time reproducibility.

### M4. The novelty claim is not demonstrated against a meaningful baseline

The related-work comparison is two short sentences contrasting the paper with intermediate-progress and paired-trace benchmarks (`paper/main.tex:119-127`). The claimed advance is a bundle of established engineering practices: frozen manifests, cohort denominators, reachability checks, parser coverage, randomized probes, and claim-evidence bookkeeping. Their combination may be useful, but usefulness is not yet methodological novelty.

There is no comparison with standard experiment tracking, observability, off-policy evaluation, or deployment provenance; no ablation showing which audit-contract elements catch errors that a conventional pipeline misses; and no second system showing transfer. The manuscript already calls transfer a hypothesis (`paper/main.tex:337-340`), which is appropriately cautious. A reviewer can still reject because the remaining contribution is an unusually transparent case report rather than a validated new method. The remedy is either a concrete baseline/ablation or a narrower case-study positioning.

### M5. The randomized clone interval needs explicit sequential-analysis labeling

The clone experiment is correctly described as hash-randomized, inconclusive, and failed under its frozen decision rule (`paper/main.tex:285-291`, `316-321`). That is a substantial correction over causal overreach. The remaining issue is that the displayed "95%" interval is unadjusted after an earlier interim look recorded in the experiment evidence. Saying only "unadjusted" does not tell every reader that nominal repeated-look coverage is not 95%.

Label the interval "nominal descriptive interval after two looks," identify the first look, and avoid any frequentist coverage implication. Also state in the same paragraph that the released artifact cannot reconstruct assignment because game identifiers and salts are withheld. This is a wording and artifact limitation, not evidence that the point estimate was fabricated.

## Objections that did not survive scrutiny

- **"The paper optimizes expected money instead of the scored objective."** Rejected. The source derives same-cell, same-role empirical rank, separates raw per-game rank from the undisclosed final aggregation, and gives a verified finite counterexample where more expected money yields less expected rank (`paper/main.tex:90-117`). The theory verifier passed the corresponding identities.

- **"The paper presents midranks or the full rating equation as official."** Rejected. It explicitly says official tie handling is undocumented and uses midranks only locally; it conditions the displayed update on fixed opponent-strength and public step-size assumptions (`paper/main.tex:96-113`).

- **"The 960 live grid is confused with the 1,320-row benchmark."** Rejected. The current text separates them and gives both counts (`paper/main.tex:90-94`).

- **"Completed-deal survivorship drives the headline empirical result."** Rejected for the claims currently made. The 63-game final-era census retains all joined/completed games, including zeros, walks, no-deals, and unresolved outcomes (`paper/main.tex:204-251`). The clone denominator includes all 91 assigned games. The paper no longer treats completed-only agreement prices as causal evidence.

- **"Observational chronology and replay are sold as randomized causal effects."** Rejected. The chronology is labeled observational, the clone is separately labeled randomized and inconclusive, and the hidden-state replay is called retrospective, selected, and unreproduced (`paper/main.tex:285-302`).

- **"The 4/4 parser misses establish a population failure rate."** Rejected. The current abstract and behavior section use them as a bounded counterexample to the strict parser, not a prevalence or effect estimate (`paper/main.tex:74-84`, `221-229`).

- **"The endpoint is attributed to the final policy or to a successful ablation."** Rejected. The paper states best-of-five sequential selection, reports the small final-era rating weight, and says no live-reached intervention passed its efficacy rule (`paper/main.tex:132-149`, `275-283`).

- **"The named bargaining, negotiation, and persuasion rules do not match frozen code."** Not supported by the checked evidence. The submitted-bank manifest and policy assets verified against current source hashes, and the five theory/code verification groups passed. The paper also limits its prose to selected owner branches rather than claiming a complete policy specification (`paper/main.tex:151-202`). This does not prove deployment receipt, but a direct prose-code mismatch is not a defensible rejection ground on the inspected branches.

- **"The account's five profiles violate the one-account rule."** Rejected. The official materials permit updating an agent; the manuscript describes sequential policy eras within one participant account, not parallel owned agents (`docs/reference/glee-docs.md:527-538`; `paper/main.tex:132-149`).

- **"The aggregate artifact necessarily creates a privacy breach."** Not established. Raw messages, identities, timestamps, game IDs, and salts are withheld; the closed-allowlist and privacy checks passed. The unresolved issue is the external ethics/data-use determination, not evidence of an exposed secret in the inspected bundle.
