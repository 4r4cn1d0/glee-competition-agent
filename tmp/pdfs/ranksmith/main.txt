            Winning the Percentile, Not the Payoff:
     A Deterministic, Experiment-Driven Agent for GLEE

                                                                Abstract
 1               GLEE evaluates agents in bargaining, bilateral negotiation, and repeated persuasion,
 2               but its competition objective is not expected payoff: each outcome is ranked against
 3               payoffs from the exact configuration and role before entering an opponent-adjusted
 4               rating. We present R ANK S MITH, a deterministic, LLM-free agent built from game-
 5               theoretic structure, empirical field models, and a safety layer that makes every
 6               decision total and auditable. The main lesson from development is methodological.
 7               Several apparently strong changes vanished or reversed after retaining all assigned
 8               outcomes, defining cohorts before treatment, checking that a flag changed the
 9               submitted action, and auditing the final action sent to the server. At a protected
10               August 26 snapshot, our best agent had an overall displayed rating of 2562, with
11               family ratings 2542/2732/2413 for bargaining/negotiation/persuasion. We describe
12               the agent, report structural findings from live behavior, and show how rank-aware
13               experimentation prevented selection bias and invalid replay assumptions from
14               becoming policy.


15   1     Introduction
16   GLEE standardizes two-player language-based economic games spanning alternating-offer bargaining,
17   bilateral trade, and Bayesian persuasion [Shapira et al., 2024]. The live competition adds a distinctive
18   objective: a payoff y is converted to an empirical percentile q = Fc,r (y) within the exact configuration
19   c and role r, adjusted for opponent strength, and mapped to a per-game rating
                                     G = 2000 + 8000(q − 0.5),             ∆R = η(G − R),                       (1)
20   with a decaying step size η [GLEE Competition Organizing Team, 2026]. Consequently, an outcome
21   that maximizes dollars need not maximize rank. A zero can be median in a structurally infeasible cell
22   but bottom-decile in a cell where others trade; moving just above an atom in the payoff distribution
23   can be worth more than a much larger dollar improvement elsewhere.
24   We built R ANK S MITH around this distinction. The online policy is deliberately conventional: exact
25   or finite-horizon game-theoretic structure where available, conservative empirical floors where the
26   field violates equilibrium, and deterministic posterior updates under hidden information. The less
27   conventional contribution is the development loop. Every proposed change was treated as a falsifiable
28   mechanism with a recoverable assignment, an all-outcome intent-to-treat cohort, and a wire-level
29   audit. This mattered because the largest apparent gains were often measurement failures rather than
30   strategy gains.
31   Our contributions are: (i) a deterministic, LLM-free agent covering all three GLEE families; (ii)
32   structural live-field measurements that localize where performance is and is not recoverable; and (iii)
33   an agent-behavior analysis protocol that detects treatment leakage, unreachable gates, survivorship
34   bias, and fabricated outcomes before deployment.

35   2     Agent design
36   Total deterministic dispatcher. A single dispatcher selects a family policy, records its numerical
37   plan and fired gates, coerces the result into the server schema, and applies final postconditions.
38   Any exception degrades to a legal family-specific fallback instead of allowing a turn timeout. The
39   submitted action, rather than an intermediate plan, is the unit audited in replay. The final protected
40   agent used no neural model or LLM in its decision or message path.

41   Bargaining. For k rounds remaining, the proposer share follows the finite recursion f (1) = 1 and
42   f (k) = 1 − δresp f (k − 1), approaching the Rubinstein solution under an infinite horizon [Rubinstein,
43   1982]. The agent blends this value with the field’s strong 50/50 anchor, floors offers at a level the
44   field actually accepts, and estimates continuation from the opponent’s observed concession trail. A
45   role audit over 4,001 games found a severe initial asymmetry (Alice 71.5th percentile, Bob 28.8th);
46   an accept-floor correction made Bob the stronger seat in a later 500-game export.

47   Negotiation. With both values visible, the agent computes the zone of possible agreement (ZOPA)
48   exactly and follows an interior reservation path. With values hidden, it uses role-specific anchors,
49   Boulware concession, table-based acceptance, support bounds from the public value grid, and terminal
50   rules. Structural inspection showed that all 6 complete-information value cells are feasible, while only
51   6 of 16 hidden-information cells are; thus 10 of 22 grid cells are infeasible. This prevents treating
52   every no-deal as a policy failure.

53   Persuasion. The seller conditions recommendations on quality, prior expected value, and observed
54   demand. The buyer maintains a discrete posterior over seller low-quality recommendation rates
55   q ∈ {0, .125, .25, .5, .75, 1}, updates it from the full recommendation stream, and purchases when
56   posterior expected utility clears price. This is an adaptive analogue of the sender-receiver structure
57   in Bayesian persuasion [Kamenica and Gentzkow, 2011]. Text is parsed into positive, negative, or
58   abstaining evidence; explicit declines are hard vetoes in the final policy.

59   3     Rank-aware development and evaluation
60   Figure 1 summarizes the contract used for uncertain changes. Behavioral changes landed default-
61   off. When randomization was authorized, a stable hash of game ID assigned treatment per game;
62   assignment occurred after eligibility was defined and was kept out of unrelated decision variables.
63   Otherwise, we used a frozen named challenger and reported the comparison as observational.
64   The primary cohort was defined at the first structurally eligible pre-action turn and retained agreements,
65   no-deals, walks, invalids, timeouts, immediate accepts, and unresolved results. Missing finals
66   remained unresolved and blocked promotion. The primary metric was authenticated official per-game
67   rating delta when joinable; a checked-in exact-cell payoff CDF supplied a diagnostic percentile. We
68   never promoted on completed deals alone.
69   Reachability was a separate requirement: every mechanism had to show positive counts for eligible
70   states, firings, and changed wire actions under the intended live stack. We then re-ran the flag-off and
71   flag-on paths and compared the post-coercion action. This catches a clean-looking null from a flag
72   nested under a disabled parent, as well as an apparent treatment that an action sanitizer later erases.

76    The more transferable result is the number of attractive changes we rejected. A replay sweep
77    suggested that leaving only 10%of visible negotiation surplus to the opponent gained +0.0121
78    diagnostic percentile across four runs. Audit showed that unseen aggressive asks were accepted by
79    the clone by construction, response bins were too coarse, hidden-value tables were populated mainly
80    by agreements, and one flag changed three distinct quantities. We rejected the entire result.
81    Likewise, accepting every profitable hidden offer first appeared to gain +0.07138 per exposed
82    decision. That counterfactual treated every observed rejection as a zero. Replaying the actual
83    later continuation reversed the result to −0.06628 (95%bootstrap CI [−0.08183, −0.05096]) per
84    exposed decision and −0.00614 over the 4,050-game cohort. A leader-trajectory clone produced an
85    encouraging early official lift of +1.812 rating/game on 45 games, but the fixed 91-game checkpoint
86    fell to +0.869 with CI [−0.78, 2.52], raised no-deals by 6.14 points, and was negative on one host; it
 87   failed its frozen promotion gates.
88    Finally, an outcome reader once imputed old missing finals as 5th-percentile abandonments. The
89    collector had actually stopped with 11,737 results outstanding. Treating these records as unresolved
90    rather than losses changed which experiments were even readable. These failures explain why our
91    final policy contains fewer experimental mechanisms than the number of promising development
 92   plots might suggest.

93    5   Agent Behavior Analysis
94    We define alignment operationally: the visible state should select the intended family plan; a gate
95    should fire only in its documented scope; coercion should preserve the intended semantic action;
96    and the wire should be attributable to exactly one mechanism. Every turn log therefore stores the
97    state fingerprint, plan, ordered gate trace, source, and submitted action. Paired reachability checks
98    establish that a mechanism can change behavior before any outcome analysis, while independent
99    readers recompute assignment and expected wires rather than trusting logged treatment labels.
100   This process found behavioral mismatches that aggregate score could not. A parser bought messages
101   containing “Not recommended” because the positive token matched first; the corrected strict decline
102   gates are limited to current-message buyer decisions and can only change buy to pass. A surplus-share
103   probe randomized an offer target before the action branch, unintentionally changing accept/reject


                                                           3
104   decisions even on turns that sent no offer. The probe was disabled because its outcome could not
105   identify the intended price-response mechanism. In another case, a replay candidate replaced the
106   entire control flag set instead of overlaying one change, silently measuring two strategies rather than
107   one parameter.
108   The final Agent 5 snapshot had all per-game A/B flags explicitly off and used deterministic templates.
109   This does not prove semantic alignment for arbitrary language or opponents; it shows that the
110   deployed policy matched its encoded design under the audited GLEE action interface.

111   6   Limitations and conclusion
112   The live field is nonstationary, opponents adapt, and identical code showed roughly ±125 rating
113   dispersion in persuasion over ten hours. Hidden opponent values are never revealed, so per-game
114   feasibility and many rejected-offer counterfactuals are not identifiable from our logs. Several full-
115   stack comparisons are observational because the operator later prohibited live randomization. Our text
116   findings concern this parser-heavy competition population and do not establish that natural language
117   is generally useless. The August 26 rating is a dated snapshot, not a final placement.
118   Within those limits, R ANK S MITH demonstrates a practical principle for economic agents: optimize
119   the scored rank, but distrust every measured gain until the treatment changes the final action and
120   every assigned outcome is retained. In this competition, the discipline to reject false wins was itself a
121   competitive advantage.
